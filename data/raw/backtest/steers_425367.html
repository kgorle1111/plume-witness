Cause of Emission Event or Excess Opacity Event, or Reason for Scheduled Activity:
The SAS processing unit at the NCTI plant had been shut down for planned maintenance. Operators were in the process of returning benzene process equipment to service following the planned maintenance. While lining up a benzene product tank, a valve that had been used as a process bleed to prepare the equipment for the planned maintenance (bleed valve) was inadvertently left in the open position. When an operator opened the line to the tank, benzene flowed to the open bleed valve, and approximately 1.5 gallons or 12 lbs of benzene was released to the SAS process containment area. The operator immediately shut the tank valve and then shut the bleed valve to stop such benzene release. Immediately after that, operators began taking steps to clean up as much as possible of the spilled benzene in the SAS process containment area, and to apply microbe-based liquid and soaps to remove remaining benzene. However, due to the atmospheric conditions of the day, a portion of the benzene released to containment area evaporated to the atmosphere.

Even though NCTI estimated the quantity of benzene that was released to containment area based on highly conservative estimations, NCTI could not accurately estimate the quantity of benzene that was evaporated to the atmosphere. Therefore, NCTI does not know if the quantity of benzene that was evaporated to the atmosphere exceeded the reportable quantity for benzene, or, thus, whether the emissions event was a reportable emissions event. However, NCTI conservatively chose to treat the emissions event as a reportable emissions event, and NCTI reported it according to 30 TAC § 101.201.

NCTI conducted an incident investigation and determined the following. Two operators were working together to remove lockout tagout devices and return benzene processing equipment to service following the planned maintenance. One of the operators was called off of the task, leaving the other operator working alone. Rather than review the lockout/tagout documentation to determine if the bleed valve had been closed, the remaining operator incorrectly assumed the other operator had already closed the bleed valve. Additionally, neither operator conducted a pressure test on the equipment prior to lining it up to live process.

In response to the findings of this incident investigation, NCTI will update lockout/tagout documentation to require documentation of a successful pressure test of process equipment prior to returning the process to live service. Additionally, NCTI will conduct refresher training with all affected operators and other appropriate employees to help ensure this type of incident does not recur.
Actions Taken, or Being Taken, to Minimize Emissions And/or Correct the Situation:
