Cause of Emission Event or Excess Opacity Event, or Reason for Scheduled Activity:
RV-4107 was being pulled for relief valve 3 year preventative maintenance in the early evening of 7/8/24. While lining up RV-4106, the parallel spare RV to RV-4107, RV-4107 lifted and released material to the flare header. Hurricane Beryl touched down in the southern part of the Texas coast on the morning of 7/8/24. The Styrolution site was affected from the early morning of 7/8/24 to the early afternoon.
This RV is located on the top deck of the reactor structure. The RVs (4107 and 4106) were impacted by the full force of the hurricane winds during 7/8/24. These RV are pilot operated and are sensitive to harmonics and vibrations.
Actions Taken, or Being Taken, to Minimize Emissions And/or Correct the Situation:
Initial response was to pull feed to the vessel and isolate both relief valves to stop the excess emissions from the flare. Once safe operation was maintained on 7/9/2024, RV-4106 was put into service and was not leaking by to the flare. Fenceline monitoring was performed at the request of the TCEQ on 7/10/2024. Fence line monitoring resulted in 0 ppm measurements for nearly every instance during the monitoring window.
Basis Used to Determine Quantities and Any Additional Information Necessary to Evaluate the Event:
Use of online flow, pressure, temperature, concentration analyzers, environmental emission software, and process knowledge to determine material sent to the flare.
