Cause of Emission Event or Excess Opacity Event, or Reason for Scheduled Activity:
Weather at the plant (Pasadena, Texas) was unusually cold on the morning of February 20, 2025. During the cold weather, the plant had a power outage that interrupted flare monitoring instruments. The routine manufacturing activities were shut down to minimize emissions. The plant's ethylene pipeline is equipped with a pressure safety relief valve (PSV). As the weather temperature rose, the pressure in the pipeline increased and the PSV directed ethylene to the flare. The flare supplemental fuel was active and the pilots were lit. Net heating value of the flare gas is unavailable. Based on HRVOC regulations, the destruction efficiency is assumed to be 93% [30 TAC 115.725(d)(7)]. Based on preliminary calculations, the resulting estimated unauthorized emissions are 100 pounds ethylene. The excess emissions began at approximately 10:20 am on February 20, 2025.

***UPDATE 03/04/2025***

Upon further investigation, there were no unauthorized emissions.
Actions Taken, or Being Taken, to Minimize Emissions And/or Correct the Situation:
When Ketjen became aware of the release, the vent was changed to direct the ethylene to a process vessel to store it rather than directing it to the flare.
The emissions were directed to a flare with 93% destruction efficiency. An investigation has been started and is in progress. The emissions in this initial report are preliminary. An update to the emissions estimate will be provided at the end of the investigation.
Basis Used to Determine Quantities and Any Additional Information Necessary to Evaluate the Event:
This event would not have resulted in excess emissions during normal flare operation. The power outage that interrupted flare monitoring may have been caused by unusually cold weather. Engineering estimates are used to report preliminary emissions. The emissions were routed to a flare. The flare pilots were lit and destruction efficiency is assumed to be 93%. The total upset emissions for the period 02/20/2025 through 02/21/2025 are estimated to be at 100 pounds ethylene. The average 24-hour emission was approximately 100 pounds. The RQ for ethylene in Harris County is 100 pounds.

***UPDATE 03/04/2025***

Upon further investigation, there were no unauthorized emissions. Additionally, the maximum hourly rate was approximately 22.7 pounds of ethylene. This is below the maximum allowable ethylene emissions for this source. The total emissions were estimated to be approximately 62.8 pounds over approximately 4 hours. No RQ was exceeded. Power was restored at approximately 2:38 PM on 02/21/2025 and the flare returned to normal operation.
