Generated by `uv run python -m pipeline.backtest --region beaumont` from `data/raw/region_beaumont/`; full output in

## Key results (each with its denominator)

1. **Downwind:** in 37 / 81 (46%; 95% CI 35-56%) of the company-reported releases, a state monitor within 15 km that had data that day was downwind of the facility at some hour from 1 h before to 6 h after the reported start (monitors without data that day add none).
2. **Recall at the current trigger (10x/10ppb):** 0 / 81 (0%; 95% CI 0-4%) releases had a trigger hit near the reported start; of the 37 with a downwind monitor, 0 hit at a monitor that was downwind that hour or the hour before.
3. **Control:** on ordinary days (same monitors, same hours, same weekday, 7/14/21 days either side, no candidate that day), the same trigger hits in 0 / 413 (0%; 95% CI 0-1%) of windows. At no setting in the sweep is the release-day rate clearly above the control rate (the 95% intervals overlap at every setting), so **hourly monitors at these triggers cannot tell these releases apart from ordinary days at the same hours.**
4. **Lowering the trigger:** the most sensitive setting, 3x / 1 ppb, hits 1 of 81 releases and 18 of 413 control windows, at 2.5 alerts a week across the 2 monitors (current: 0.0 a week). Across all 9 settings, the release-day rate minus the control rate runs from -4 to +0 percentage points: a lower trigger fires more often on release days and on ordinary days alike.
5. **Small releases:** 29 of 81 releases reported under 10 lb of the monitored compound.
6. **Calm wind:** 1 / 2 (50%; 95% CI 10-90%) current-trigger alerts with a wind speed reading (2 alerts in all) came in wind under 3 mph at the detecting monitor in the detection hour, where the upwind direction is unreliable. The record check's calm rule also looks at the hour before (the lower of the two), so it abstains on more spikes than this count; `docs/backtest-beaumont.md` gives that number.

## How to read this

- A control window is the release's own window moved by 7, 14 or 21 days (same weekday), kept only inside
  2024-2025 and never on a day that is any candidate's start day: 413 windows. The comparison asks
  whether a release day looks different from an ordinary day at the same place and hour. It does not prove that a
  hit on a release day came from the release.
- Downwind on control days uses that day's wind, so the control "downwind" columns compare like with like.
- "Separable" means the two 95% Wilson intervals do not overlap, a deliberately conservative test with this few
  events.

## Limits

- **Start day only.** Hours after midnight are not looked at, so a release after ~18:00 CST loses part of its
  window.
- **Reported start times can be off.** The window is anchored on the company's reported start; a late or rounded
  start moves the window.
- **ECHO coordinates.** Facility positions are EPA ECHO points, which can sit hundreds of meters from the
  emission point; the wedge test uses them as given.
- **Wind source.** Every site has met-form wind; a site with none on a day uses the nearest site's within 10 km.
- **Geometry, not dispersion.** "Downwind" is the corridor's wedge (+/-22.5 deg, 15 km), not a plume model; a
  downwind monitor can still miss a narrow or lofted plume.
- **Alerts per week is a false-alarm proxy, not a false-alarm rate:** nobody knows the true cause of each alert.
- The candidate count comes from `backtest.select_candidates`; a change to the report parser that adds candidates
  changes every denominator. AutoGC data are provisional.
