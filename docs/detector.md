## Sentences a pitch can use (each with its denominator)

1. **Downwind:** in 52 / 72 (72%; 95% CI 61-81%) of the company-reported releases, a state monitor within 15 km that had data that day was downwind of the facility at some hour from 1 h before to 6 h after the reported start (52 of 72 counting monitors without data that day). "Most releases never reach a state monitor" is not supported by this geometry.
2. **Recall at the current trigger (10x/10ppb):** 3 / 72 (4%; 95% CI 1-12%) releases had a trigger hit near the reported start; of the 52 with a downwind monitor, 1 hit at a monitor that was downwind that hour or the hour before.
3. **Control:** on ordinary days (same monitors, same hours, same weekday, 7/14/21 days either side, no candidate that day), the same trigger hits in 19 / 386 (5%; 95% CI 3-8%) of windows. At no setting in the sweep is the release-day rate clearly above the control rate (the 95% intervals overlap at every setting), so **hourly monitors at these triggers cannot tell these releases apart from ordinary days at the same hours.**
4. **Lowering the trigger:** the most sensitive setting, 3x / 1 ppb, hits 39 of 72 releases and 219 of 386 control windows, at 13.2 alerts a week across the 8 monitors (current: 4.0 a week). Across all 9 settings, the release-day rate minus the control rate runs from -7 to -1 percentage points: a lower trigger fires more often on release days and on ordinary days alike.
5. **Small releases:** 27 of 72 releases reported under 10 lb of the monitored compound.
6. **Calm wind:** 79 / 414 (19%; 95% CI 16-23%) current-trigger alerts with a wind speed reading (415 alerts in all) came in wind under 3 mph at the detecting monitor, where the upwind direction is unreliable.

## How to read this

- A control window is the release's own window moved by 7, 14 or 21 days (same weekday), kept only inside
  2024-2025 and never on a day that is any candidate's start day: 386 windows. The comparison asks
  whether a release day looks different from an ordinary day at the same place and hour. It does not prove that a
  hit on a release day came from the release.
- Downwind on control days uses that day's wind, so the control "downwind" columns compare like with like.
- "Separable" means the two 95% Wilson intervals do not overlap, a deliberately conservative test with this few
  events.

## Limits

- **Start day only.** Hours after midnight are not looked at, so a release after ~18:00 CST loses part of its
  window.
- **Reported start times can be off.** The window is anchored on the company's reported start; a late or rounded
  start moves the window.
- **ECHO coordinates.** Facility positions are EPA ECHO points, which can sit hundreds of meters from the
  emission point; the wedge test uses them as given.
- **Borrowed wind.** Galena Park, HRM-16 Deer Park, Clinton, HRM-3 Haden Road have no wind of their own and use the nearest site's.
- **Geometry, not dispersion.** "Downwind" is the build.py wedge (+/-22.5 deg, 15 km), not a plume model; a
  downwind monitor can still miss a narrow or lofted plume.
- **Alerts per week is a false-alarm proxy, not a false-alarm rate:** nobody knows the true cause of each alert.
- Candidate count depends on `backtest.select_candidates` on this branch; a parser fix that adds candidates
  changes every denominator. AutoGC data are provisional.
