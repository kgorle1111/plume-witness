As the weather temperature rose, the pressure in the pipeline increased and the PSV directed ethylene to the flare. The flare supplemental fuel was active and the pilots were lit. Net heating value of the flare gas is unavailable. Based on HRVOC regulations, the destruction efficiency is assumed to be 93% \[30 TAC 115.725(d)(7)\]. Based on preliminary calculations, the resulting estimated unauthorized emissions are 100 pounds ethylene. The excess emissions began at approximately 10:20 am on February 20, 2025. \*\*\*UPDATE 03/04/2025\*\*\* Upon further investigation, there were no unauthorized emissions.

**actions**

> When Ketjen became aware of the release, the vent was changed to direct the ethylene to a process vessel to store it rather than directing it to the flare. The emissions were directed to a flare with 93% destruction efficiency. An investigation has been started and is in progress. The emissions in this initial report are preliminary. An update to the emissions estimate will be provided at the end of the investigation.

**basis**

> This event would not have resulted in excess emissions during normal flare operation. The power outage that interrupted flare monitoring may have been caused by unusually cold weather. Engineering estimates are used to report preliminary emissions. The emissions were routed to a flare. The flare pilots were lit and destruction efficiency is assumed to be 93%. The total upset emissions for the period 02/20/2025 through 02/21/2025 are estimated to be at 100 pounds ethylene. The average 24-hour emission was approximately 100 pounds. The RQ for ethylene in Harris County is 100 pounds. \*\*\*UPDATE 03/04/2025\*\*\* Upon further investigation, there were no unauthorized emissions. Additionally, the maximum hourly rate was approximately 22.7 pounds of ethylene. This is below the maximum allowable ethylene emissions for this source. The total emissions were estimated to be approximately 62.8 pounds over approximately 4 hours. No RQ was exceeded. Power was restored at approximately 2:38 PM on 02/21/2025 and the flare returned to normal operation.

## 65. steers_425367

TCEQ: https://www2.tceq.texas.gov/oce/eer/index.cfm?fuseaction=main.getDetails&target=425367  
Local copy: `data/raw/backtest/steers_425367.html`

**cause**

> The SAS processing unit at the NCTI plant had been shut down for planned maintenance. Operators were in the process of returning benzene process equipment to service following the planned maintenance. While lining up a benzene product tank, a valve that had been used as a process bleed to prepare the equipment for the planned maintenance (bleed valve) was inadvertently left in the open position. When an operator opened the line to the tank, benzene flowed to the open bleed valve, and approximately 1.5 gallons or 12 lbs of benzene was released to the SAS process containment area. The operator immediately shut the tank valve and then shut the bleed valve to stop such benzene release. Immediately after that, operators began taking steps to clean up as much as possible of the spilled benzene in the SAS process containment area, and to apply microbe-based liquid and soaps to remove remaining benzene. However, due to the atmospheric conditions of the day, a portion of the benzene released to containment area evaporated to the atmosphere. Even though NCTI estimated the quantity of benzene that was released to containment area based on highly conservative estimations, NCTI could not accurately estimate the quantity of benzene that was evaporated to the atmosphere. Therefore, NCTI does not know if the quantity of benzene that was evaporated to the atmosphere exceeded the reportable quantity for benzene, or, thus, whether the emissions event was a reportable emissions event. However, NCTI conservatively chose to treat the emissions event as a reportable emissions event, and NCTI reported it according to 30 TAC § 101.201. NCTI conducted an incident investigation and determined the following. Two operators were working together to remove lockout tagout devices and return benzene processing equipment to service following the planned maintenance. One of the operators was called off of the task, leaving the other operator working alone. Rather than review the lockout/tagout documentation to determine if the bleed valve had been closed, the remaining operator incorrectly assumed the other operator had already closed the bleed valve. Additionally, neither operator conducted a pressure test on the equipment prior to lining it up to live process. In response to the findings of this incident investigation, NCTI will update lockout/tagout documentation to require documentation of a successful pressure test of process equipment prior to returning the process to live service. Additionally, NCTI will conduct refresher training with all affected operators and other appropriate employees to help ensure this type of incident does not recur.

**actions**

> Once the emissions event was identified, action was taken immediately to stop the release and return to compliance. The operator immediately shut the tank valve and then shut the bleed valve to stop such benzene release. Immediately after that, operators began taking steps to clean up as much as possible of the spilled benzene in the SAS process containment area, and to apply microbe-based liquid and soaps to remove remaining benzene.

**basis**

> The following is response to the identified criteria questions for emissions events: 1.
